# DUFYND Creative Learning Library v1

Updated: 2026-09-22
Purpose: canonical internal learning library for premium fragrance short/commercial production.

## Source-handling rule
The examples are learning references, not copy templates. Reuse production logic, pacing, shot grammar, consistency controls and editing principles. Adapt product truth, notes, materials, palette and brand language to each fragrance.

Where a source prompt was only visible inside a compressed screen-recording, treat recovered wording as partial unless separately provided verbatim by the user. Do not invent unreadable source text.

## Example 34 — Byredo Bal d'Afrique Absolu
**Primary role:** master workflow / methodology.
**Model shown:** Seedance 2.0 + Gemini Omni.
**Prompt availability:** full Step 1 / Step 2 / Step 3 / Step 3.1 supplied verbatim by user in project conversation.

### Learnings
- Character sheet / technical blueprint before video.
- Canonical @sheet identity across every product shot.
- Timecoded shotlist, not a vague cinematic prompt.
- Alternate atmosphere, ingredient macro, product hero, product detail and final hero.
- Hard cuts can feel luxurious when the individual shots are controlled.
- Text and subtle FX are separate preservation passes after the base video is already successful.
- Explicitly preserve frame rate, camera speed, timing and color in finishing passes.
- 3D-behind-subject typography is a premium editorial device.

**DUFYND usage:** structural backbone for high-value fragrance campaigns.

## Example 35 — Amouage Decision 42
**Primary role:** delayed reveal / concealment.
**Model shown:** Seedance 2.5.

### Recovered prompt logic
- `@sheet is the visual truth`.
- Intimate dark set with weathered driftwood, dark stone and polished wet/dark surfaces.
- Environment reveals gradually with the product.
- Product is deliberately not fully visible or legible in early shots.
- Exposure remains controlled and constant inside shots.
- Hard cuts only.
- Every shot uses a different framing scale/angle.

### Learnings
- Mystery works when it is designed, not when the model accidentally hides the product.
- Early abstract fragments can protect product fidelity while building desire.
- A final full reveal becomes stronger when previous shots withheld information.

**DUFYND usage:** delayed product reveal and dark-luxury intros.

## Example 36 — Chanel Coromandel
**Primary role:** storyboard-to-sequence translation + quality lock.
**Workflow shown:** GPT Astra + Higgsfield.

### Recovered prompt logic
- Translate storyboard images into sequential full-screen shots.
- Never show the board/grid/labels in the actual commercial.
- Start directly in a macro, not a wide bottle reveal.
- Explicit image-quality lock: pristine practical luxury tabletop/macro photography.
- Prohibits fake grain, bloom, crunchy sharpening, CGI sheen, plastic foliage and orange wash.
- Uses exact shot windows and a final-frame lock.
- Final hero can be held stable and pixel-consistent.
- Audio direction can be specified separately as subtle SFX.

### Learnings
- Reference boards can control a complete sequence.
- Negative quality constraints are as important as positive art direction.
- A final-frame lock is useful when a clean CTA/end state is required.

**DUFYND usage:** reference-board workflow and artifact prevention.

## Example 39 — Oud Maracujá style setup
**Primary role:** single-shot continuous camera move.
**Model shown:** Enhanced Seedance 2.0 Fast.

### Recovered prompt logic
- Keep exact glass color, label text and cap shape unchanged.
- Dense fruit/nature set with smoke/resin cues.
- Continuous dolly-in toward the bottle.
- Shallow depth of field sharpens as camera nears product.
- No camera shake, no cuts, smooth continuous motion.

### Learnings
- Not every strong reel needs many edits.
- A single coherent move can feel expensive when foreground, atmosphere and focus progression are designed.

**DUFYND usage:** alternate format for premium single-take product content.

## Example 44 — Ex Nihilo Blue Talisman
**Primary role:** music-synced abstract transition + premium negative-space art direction.
**Model shown:** Seedance 2.5.
**User rating context:** specifically identified by user as near-perfect for music and transition.

### Recovered prompt logic
- @sheet is absolute visual truth.
- Deep low-key look; roughly 80% black negative space.
- One hard light source per shot.
- Cold icy-blue / silver-white palette; champagne-gold cap is the only warm accent.
- Deliberate alternation between extreme macro and wide/product-small-in-field.
- Explicit prohibitions: no mirrored/wet reflective floor, visible fixtures, halos, lens flares, or full bright centered bottle in every shot.
- Timecoded sequence includes:
  - near-silhouette bottle edge
  - citrus peel/oil macro
  - white blossom macro
  - glass/base macro
  - pear/dew macro
  - perfume strand/droplet macro
  - abstract light/wood beats
  - product emergence
  - vapor/cloud atmosphere
  - final hero

### Audio / transition analysis
- Approximate detected musical pulse: ~89 BPM.
- Strong onset aligns around the visually important transition near ~7.4 s.
- The transition is not primarily an overlay effect: an abstract vertical-light shot functions as a bridge between two scenes.
- This makes the transition feel authored inside the campaign rather than pasted on afterward.

### Learnings
- Design transition shots as part of the storyboard.
- Cut them on strong musical onsets.
- Use negative space and one dominant light source for premium restraint.
- One strong audiovisual bridge can define the reel.

**DUFYND usage:** key reference for Bois Impérial transition design and audio-edit discipline.

## Example 46 — YSL-style Tuxedo/Patchouli campaign
**Primary role:** strict text/label lock + 4K macro grammar.
**Model shown:** Seedance 2.0 4K.

### Recovered prompt logic
- 15 seconds, 15 shots, hard cuts only.
- Explicit bottle-proportion, juice-color, cap-finish and badge-design locks.
- Extremely detailed BADGE LOCK.
- Extremely detailed TEXT LOCK: exact wording, spacing, print treatment; no translation/restyle/invention.
- Sequence travels from cold desaturated glass/silver/black through patchouli green into oak brown/cognac amber.
- Mixes glass macro, cap/collar macro, leaf material, resin, clean product, amber glass abstraction, top-down cap and final dark hero.

### Learnings
- Text/label fidelity can be given its own dedicated lock section.
- Color progression across a reel can be intentional while product color remains fixed.
- Macro detail is most reliable when the prompt says what must *not* change.

**DUFYND usage:** strongest technical product-fidelity reference for Bois Impérial.

# Cross-example learning system

## A. Product Identity Layer
Before creative generation:
1. canonical product sheet
2. exact product views
3. geometry lock
4. material / liquid / cap lock
5. label / text lock when relevant
6. negative identity constraints

## B. Fragrance World Layer
Define:
- core palette
- light temperature
- dominant materials
- note/ingredient motifs
- environment
- forbidden visual clichés

Every supporting shot must belong to the same world.

## C. Shot Grammar Layer
Build a shot library from:
- extreme macro
- product fragment
- ingredient macro
- environment texture
- detail hero
- medium hero
- final hero

Avoid repeating the same scale and angle consecutively.

## D. Motion Layer
Default premium motion vocabulary:
- very slow push-in
- micro-drift
- slight orbit
- gentle lateral drift
- slow pull-back
- controlled rack focus
- still hold with tiny environmental movement

Use fast motion only when intentionally contrasting with the above.

## E. Reveal Layer
Options:
- immediate product hero
- delayed reveal
- fragment -> detail -> silhouette -> full hero
- ingredient -> product
- first-frame -> last-frame transformation

For premium DUFYND, delayed reveal is preferred when the product is recognizable enough to reward the payoff.

## F. Transition Layer
Priority order:
1. hard cut on music
2. designed bridge shot (Example 44)
3. first/last-frame transformation (Example 42)
4. one scoped whip/smear (Example 37)
5. conventional editing transition only if necessary

Do not scatter transitions across every cut.

## G. Audio Layer
- Select music before final timing lock when possible.
- Detect/identify strong musical onsets and align key visual cuts.
- SFX should reinforce only major physical events.
- Prefer 1-3 signature SFX to constant micro-effects.
- A transition shot + music onset usually beats a loud generic whoosh.

## H. Finishing Layer
1. lock base video first
2. text in a preservation pass
3. optional final effect in a second preservation pass
4. avoid regenerating successful shots merely to add typography

## I. Credit Discipline
- spend on better references before more generations
- one controlled premium test at a time
- reuse successful stills and shots
- build reference packs that can feed multiple videos

- exact label placement

- no invented text
- no visible lighting equipment
